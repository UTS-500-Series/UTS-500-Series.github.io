The circuit — 03

Input, output and power

A balanced receiver at the front, a balanced driver at the back, a switch that can join the two, and the supply that feeds it all.

The input

Each input leg comes off the card edge through a ferrite bead (FB2 hot, FB1 cold) and a 47 Ω resistor (R3, R1) with 470 pF to chassis (C5, C1). Bead, resistor and capacitor together keep radio frequencies out: the RC corner is about 7 MHz, far above audio. Then a 47 µF capacitor in each leg (C10, C4) blocks any DC the source carries, with 100k to ground after it (R4, R2) so the INA137’s inputs have a DC path.

IC1, the INA137, is a difference amplifier with laser-trimmed resistors inside. It subtracts the cold leg from the hot one, which cancels anything common to both (hum and noise picked up on the cable) and halves the result: −6 dB. Its output goes straight into IC4A, wired as a follower, which is what drives the first band.

Matching the input capacitors

A balanced input rejects hum only as well as its two legs match. With 47 µF coupling capacitors the corner sits well below 1 Hz, so even a 20% mismatch between C4 and C10 makes no measurable difference at 50 Hz. The parts list has them as bipolar electrolytics (Jaycar RY6820), which is right: a balanced source can sit either side of ground.

The output

The summer’s output (Po) drives IC6, the DRV134, which produces two outputs in antiphase at twice the level each side: +6 dB, undoing the receiver’s −6 dB. Its sense pins are tied to its outputs at the chip, the usual connection. From there each leg goes through SW1, gets 100 pF to ground (C36, C35) and leaves through a ferrite bead (FB3, FB4).

Counting inversions: the receiver does not invert, each band inverts once (two bands, so the signal comes out of them the right way up), the dry inverter and the summer invert once each, and the driver does not invert. The output is in phase with the input.

Bypass

SW1 is a double-pole slide switch. In one position the output pins take the DRV134’s outputs. In the other they are joined straight to the input legs, after the ferrite beads and before the 47 Ω resistors: a hard bypass, which works with the module unpowered. Both poles are wired consistently (hot to hot, cold to cold in either position).

On the board as placed so far, SW1 sits next to the card edge connector, at the back of the module. See board & parts.

Power

J1 pin 12 feeds +16V through R6 and pin 14 feeds −16V through R5, both 10 Ω. Each rail has a 100 µF reservoir (C20, C18), 1 µF (C12, C11) and 100 nF next to every chip. The 10 Ω resistors and the reservoirs form a filter against noise on the rack’s rails, as on the compressor.

From the datasheets’ typical figures, sixteen op amp channels at 1.8 mA, the INA137 at 2.4 mA and the DRV134 at 5.2 mA come to about 37 mA per rail before any load, well inside the 130 mA a 500-series slot allows.

IC2 and IC5 have no positive supply

On the sheet, the symbol on pin 4 (V+) of both IC2 and IC5 is a −16V power symbol. Pin 11 (V−) is on −16 V as well, so both chips have −16 V on both supply pins and nothing across them. IC3 and IC4 are wired correctly. KiCad’s ERC doesn’t flag it, because both pins are on a legitimate power net.

As drawn, the high-mid band (IC2), both shelf level stages, the dry inverter and the summer (all IC5) are dead, so nothing reaches the output. The fix is to change those two symbols to +16V. Their 100 nF decoupling capacitors then decouple +16 V, as they should. Do it before the board is routed, or the copper will carry the same mistake.
