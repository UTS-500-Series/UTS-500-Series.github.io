Equaliser module

The equaliser

A four-band equaliser: a low shelf, two fully parametric mid bands and a high shelf, between a balanced input receiver and a balanced line driver, with a hard bypass. The whole circuit is on one sheet with values, and the board is being laid out.

Work in progress

Written from the Equaliser repository at commit 10142c5 (7 October). The schematic was finished on 5 October; the board has its parts being placed and no tracks yet. Every response figure on these pages is worked out from the schematic itself. Nothing has been built or measured.

One wiring fault stops it working as drawn: two of the four OPA1644s have their positive supply pin on −16 V. See power.

Shelf frequencies are the corner, where the shelf reaches half its depth; their boost and cut are the most each reaches inside the audio band (at 20 Hz for the low shelf with its corner at the top of its range, at 20 kHz for the high shelf with its corner at the bottom).

The two mid bands are the state-variable circuits the team drew first, now in series on the main sheet. The shelves grew out of the separate high-pass, low-pass and gain sheets: each filter now feeds a level stage, and a summer adds or subtracts the filtered signal from the dry one. With every level knob centred the module is flat to within 0.5 dB from 20 Hz to 20 kHz.

Why the OPA1644

The filters, buffers and summer all run on TI’s OPA1644, a quad JFET-input op amp. Its input bias current is picoamps rather than the NE5532’s hundreds of nanoamps, which matters when a pot wiper sits in the signal path: bias current through a pot is offset, and offset through a pot is a click every time the knob turns. Its footprint on the sheet is TSSOP-14, a 0.65 mm-pitch surface-mount package: fine to hand-solder with flux and a fine tip, but worth knowing before ordering. The team’s parts list has the TL084 in its place, which shares the pinout; see board & parts.

The input and output use TI’s matched pair for balanced lines, the INA137 receiver and the DRV134 driver. The receiver halves the level and the driver doubles it, so the module is unity gain overall with 6 dB of extra headroom inside.
