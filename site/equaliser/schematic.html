Reading it

WhereWhat
Top leftJ2 (labelled TOP BOARD) and J3 (BOTTOM BOARD): the headers that carry the frequency pots between the two boards, pin for pin.
Top rowBalanced input receiver (IC1, the input filtering and the U1 buffer), then the low-mid band (IC3) and the high-mid band (IC2), each drawn the same way.
MiddleThe buffer (IC4B), the low-pass and high-pass filters (IC4D, IC4C), the variable gain stages (IC8A, IC8B) and the summer (IC8C, with the dry inverter IC8D).
RightThe balanced line driver (IC6) and the bypass switch (SW1).
Left and bottomIC4 and IC8 drawn as blocks with their supply pins, the mounting holes, J1 (the card edge) and the rail filtering.

Blocks talk through global labels named after the op amp unit they belong to: A+, A- and Ao are unit A’s inputs and output, and so on through P. The labels on ground (B+, C+, F+, G+, M+–P+) are right: those are + inputs that sit at ground.

About this drawing

The sheet is saved by KiCad 9. This image is drawn by the site’s own generator from the .kicad_sch file, and its netlist matches KiCad’s own export net for net. The positions, symbols and wires are the file’s; the lettering is close to KiCad’s but not identical. The PDF KiCad exports is on Design files.

That is the standard pinout, the same as the compressor’s main input and output. One difference: pins 5 and 13 are both on the one ground net here, and on the ordered board both go into the ground plane, so the rack’s supply return and audio ground are joined across the whole card. The compressor keeps them apart and joins them at one point. It is unlikely to matter on the bench; if the module hums in the rack and the others don’t, it is the first place to look.
