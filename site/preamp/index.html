The preamp module

A microphone preamplifier for the desk: balanced mic input, switchable phantom power, and enough clean gain to bring a dynamic microphone up to line level.

Nothing here is designed yet

The preamp module has no schematic, no netlist and no bill of materials. This section is a template that mirrors the structure of the compressor documentation, so the shape of the work is visible before the work starts. Every figure below is deliberately blank rather than guessed.

The preamp is the first module in the chain and sets the noise floor for everything after it. That makes its input stage the part that matters most: the compressor can be rebuilt without touching the desk's noise performance, but the preamp cannot.

What has to be decided first

These are the choices that determine everything else. Until they are made, the pages in this section have nothing to say.

DecisionWhy it drives the rest
Transformer or electronic inputSets the cost, the size, the noise floor and the whole shape of the input sheet.
Where 48 V comes fromGenerating it on-module means a converter and its noise; taking it from the rack means the chassis wiring has to carry it, which today it does not.
Gain range and how it is setA switched attenuator and a continuous pot lead to completely different circuits.
Whether the desk needs matched channelsStereo work needs channel-to-channel gain matching, which forces switched gain and tolerance parts.

How this section will be built

The compressor section is generated from a single source of truth: a design.py holding the netlist as data, which the KiCad schematic is verified against pin by pin. The interactive schematics, the component data behind them and the bill-of-materials counts all come from that one file.

This module follows the same route. Nothing here becomes real documentation until there is a design.py to generate it from.
