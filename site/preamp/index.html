Preamp module

The microphone preamp

A discrete, balanced microphone preamp: two transistor pairs doing the gain, one op amp turning it into a line output, switchable 48 V phantom power, and a single gain control between the two halves.

Where this comes from

Everything in this section is read from the team's own KiCad project in UTS-500-Series/Pre-Amp at 5e6a308. The schematic on these pages is drawn from that file and the connections are its netlist. The sheet has no component values yet, so nothing here states one.

What it does

A microphone delivers millivolts; the desk wants line level. The preamp supplies that gain while adding as little noise as it can, because everything after it — the compressor, the equaliser — can only ever be as quiet as what the preamp hands on.

The input stage is discrete: a PNP and an NPN transistor per leg, joined as a compound (Sziklai) pair. The two pairs form a differential stage, with the gain network floating between them. An NE5532 then takes the difference of the two pair outputs and drives the output.

Its lineage

The topology — Sziklai input pairs with a floating gain control into a difference amp — is the one Rod Elliott published as ESP Project 66, and the phantom feed and input protection follow the scheme in ESP Project 96. These pages document our drawing of it, with our reference designators, not ESP's.
