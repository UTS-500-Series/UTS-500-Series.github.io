Practical

The boards

The compressor is two boards. The main board is the card that plugs into the rack; a small front board sits behind the faceplate and carries everything you touch or read. A 30-way ribbon joins them. Both are laid out, routed and pass DRC, and neither has been ordered yet.

Why two boards

In a 500-series module the card stands at right angles to the faceplate, so a pot mounted on the card would point its shaft at the rack's side wall. Putting every panel part on a board of its own, parallel to the panel, lets the pots and toggles stand straight up through their holes and makes the hole positions a property of one board. The panel drawing now takes its coordinates from the front board rather than the other way round.

The panel parts stay drawn in the main schematic, so the circuit still reads as one piece. They are excluded from the main board and its parts list and drawn again, pin for pin and with the same net names, in the front board's own schematic.

The main board

Parts are grouped by section, following the signal:

  • Power entry sits by edge pins 12 to 14. +16V, -16V and -5V1 run on the top layer in 0.6 mm tracks to each chip, with decoupling against pins 8 and 4 of every op amp.
  • Grounds stay separate, as the power page explains. PGND is a single track from pin 13 to R48, CHASSIS stays by pin 1, and AGND is a pour on the bottom layer with every pad also tied in by its own track.
  • The input and output stages sit next to the edge fingers, the gain cell in the middle, and the sidechain towards the front, next to the ribbon header.
  • The matched pair and quad (Q1/Q2 and Q6–Q9) are placed touching, flat faces together, so they can be glued and stay at one temperature.
  • All seven op amps are socketed. That lets U4, a TL072, be swapped for an OPA2134 (see the sidechain) and makes a damaged chip a swap rather than a desoldering job.
  • The trimmers RV1, RV7 and RV8 stay on the main board. They are set once at bring-up.
  • The film capacitors have footprints for real parts. C5, C8, C15 (10 µF) and C9, C10 (4.7 µF) are TDK B32562 boxes on a 15 mm pitch; C14 and C35 (2.2 µF) are KEMET MMK5 on 5 mm. Nothing that size fits the original 5 mm outlines, so the board was re-placed and re-routed around them on 2 October.

The front edge of the board is set back 24 mm from the panel to clear the front board, its header and the ribbon plug.

KiCad's DRC only checks the board against its own schematic, so it can't catch a wiring slip in the schematic itself. The schematic is also compared net by net against the reference netlist, tools/design.py. That comparison caught a wire joining Q2's collector to the gain cell's tail (Q3, R14, R15), which was fixed and re-routed on 1 October.

The front board

  • Pots: 9 mm vertical, on the Alps RK09K footprint, for THRESHOLD, RATIO, ATTACK, RELEASE and MAKEUP. Altronics' 9 mm pots (R1946 and family) share its pins and lugs, and the parts list uses them: 100 kΩ log for THRESHOLD, 100 kΩ for RATIO, 10 kΩ for ATTACK and MAKEUP and 1 MΩ for RELEASE. The RK09K itself stops at 100 kΩ.
  • Toggles: Altronics' Salecom mini toggles, the S1315 SPDT for HPF, KEY and LINK and the S1350 DPDT for BYPASS. The S1332 centre-off fits the SPDT footprint if you want one.
  • Meters: twenty 2 mm LEDs in two columns of ten on a 3.0 mm pitch, with the two LM3914s (U9, U10) either side. The driver chips are soldered rather than socketed, because a socket would overlap the LEDs.
  • On the back: the ribbon header J1 and C38, which is too tall for the panel side.

The pot bodies stand 10.6 mm off the board and the toggles 10.4 mm, so the board sits about 10.6 mm behind the panel, with the pots resting against it. The LEDs need spacers of about 8 mm to reach their holes.

Joining and fixing them

  • Ribbon. 30-way, 2.54 mm pitch, with an IDC socket crimped on each end. It carries 24 signals with ground between the audio pairs. The main board's J2 is a shrouded header but the front board's J1 is a plain one, so it is not keyed: put the red stripe on pin 1 at both ends (pin 1 is marked on the silkscreen). Altronics' P1023, a 150 mm 30-way socket-to-socket strip, does the same job with no crimping.
  • Ribbon length. About 150 mm, folded once. It depends on where the board sits in your rack, so lay the boards out before crimping.
  • Front board to panel: just the nine bushing nuts.
  • Main board to panel: an L-bracket (25 × 25 mm aluminium angle, or a short angle on standoffs) from the back of the panel to the two M3 holes H1 and H2 near the board's front edge. H2 is plated and tied to CHASSIS, so the bracket also grounds the panel. Mask the anodise where the bracket touches it.

The faceplate fit guide covers the same joint for every module.

3D models

Every part has a 3D model, so KiCad's 3D viewer and a STEP export for the Fusion 360 assembly show both boards complete. The pots use Alps' own RK09K model, which is not kept in the repository: run sh tools/get_3d_models.sh once to download it. The toggles use simple models of the Salecom parts. The renders on this site stand a plain block and shaft in for the pots, because Alps' download is out of reach where they are made.

Check before ordering boards

  • The main board needs tidying by hand. It was re-placed and re-routed by script for the film capacitors. It passes DRC with nothing unconnected, but the TIMING node and Q3's emitter run longer than they need to, and no one has reviewed the routing yet.
  • Panel thickness. A 3.18 mm panel leaves the pots under 2 mm of thread: counterbore the pot holes or use a thinner panel (front panel).
  • Pot direction. If pin 3 is the clockwise end, THRESHOLD and RATIO turn the opposite way to the panel legend (see the simulation).
  • Ribbon length is an estimate until the boards are in a rack.
